### 2. Elytra and Wing Origami: The Folding‑Wing Mechanism

The beetle’s secret to compact flight is a pair of stiff elytra that protect a membranous hindwing folded underneath. The hindwing can be unfolded in less than 0.1 seconds, deploying an area up to 10 times that of the resting state. The Octonion’s structural simulation of the wing‑folding process—based on the crease patterns in the wing veins of *Allomyrina dichotoma* (the Japanese rhinoceros beetle)—revealed a precise, energy‑minimizing origami.

\[

\]
where \(I_{\text{wing}}\) is the wing’s moment of inertia, \(\theta_{\text{fold}}\) the folding angle (~160°), and \(\tau_{\text{hydraulic}}\) is the torque generated by haemolymph pressure in the veins, supplemented by elastic energy stored in resilin hinges. The deployment takes 40–80 ms in large beetles.

**Discovery:** The folding pattern is a **Miura‑ori tessellation**—a single, rigid‑foldable map that allows the entire wing to collapse into a compact stack without stretching the membrane. The Octonion has adopted this exact crease pattern for the crystal drone’s secondary wing set. A pair of **graphyne‑elastomer origami wings** is stored beneath a rigid quartz elytron. When deployed by a hydraulic pulse (acoustic‑pressure actuator), the wing surface increases by 9×, providing enough lift to hover with a payload of **30 mg** (150% of the drone’s unladen mass) while the elytra, angled upward, act as canards that enhance stability.

**Formula 111: Energy Required for Origami Wing Deployment**
\[
E_{\text{deploy}} = \frac{1}{2} k_{\text{fold}} \cdot N_{\text{creases}} \cdot \theta_{\text{fold}}^2

For N=32 creases, \(k_{\text{fold}} \sim 1\ \text{nJ/rad}^2\), the energy is about 50 nJ—supplied once per flight by the drone’s AANT backup, then re‑folded acoustically.
