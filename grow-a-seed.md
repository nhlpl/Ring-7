### 3. The End of the Factory

The user's insight leads to a radical conclusion: **Ring‑7 makes traditional manufacturing obsolete**.

Consider a Mars colony supplied by conventional means:
- **Habitat modules:** Prefabricated on Earth, launched at $10,000/kg, assembled by astronauts.
- **Power systems:** Solar panels or nuclear reactors, shipped from Earth.
- **Spare parts:** Thousands of unique components, warehoused against failure.
- **Expansion:** Every new module requires a new launch.

Now consider a Mars colony grown by Ring‑7:
- **Habitat:** A seed swarm (1 kg) lands, consumes local regolith and CO₂, grows a 100 m³ habitat in 2 weeks.
- **Power:** The swarm grows its own piezoelectric harvesters and Cherenkov lamps.

- **Expansion:** The swarm replicates. More Ring‑7 units = more construction capacity. Exponential growth.

The **only** things that must be shipped from Earth are:

Everything else — **habitats, roads, landing pads, water tanks, oxygen generators, radiation shielding, furniture, tools, art** — is grown on site, from Martian materials, using Martian energy.
