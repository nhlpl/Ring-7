### 2. Flight Mechanics: Four‑Wing Supremacy & Unmatched Agility

The emperor dragonfly’s flight is the benchmark against which all other aerial robots are measured. - **Body mass:** 1.0–1.2 g
- **Flapping frequency:** 30–40 Hz (variable)
- **Wing stroke amplitude:** Can vary from ±20° (hovering) to ±80° (high‑speed pursuit)
- **Reynolds number:** ~5,000 (transitional, but with highly stable attached vortices)

The Octonion’s CFD analysis has isolated this “**Dynamic Asymmetric Clap (DAC)**” as the primary mechanism for its acute evasive and predatory manoeuvres.

\]
where \(\Delta C_L(\theta)\) is the instantaneous lift‑coefficient difference between the clapping and non‑clapping wing pairs, \(b\) the wingspan, and \(\Gamma(\Delta\phi)\) a phase‑synchronisation factor that maximizes the jet’s asymmetry.

\[

where \(w_i\) is a learned weighting that enhances small, erratic trajectories, and \(\sigma_{\text{clutter}}\) is the motion noise from the background landscape.

### 4. Interception Algorithm: The Constant‑Bearing Missile of the Pond

The emperor dragonfly does not chase its prey. It hunts with the same deadly mathematical elegance as a peregrine falcon, steering to maintain a constant bearing to its target. This “constant bearing, decreasing range” (CBDR) strategy is the basis of modern proportional navigation, and the dragonfly executes it with a neural circuit that is both provably optimal and stunningly efficient.

\[

\]

It will also actively adjust \(N\) in real time to account for target acceleration, using a predictive Kalman‑like filter implemented in a mere handful of neurons.

**Discovery:** The dragonfly does not calculate the math; it has a single, identified interneuron that fires at a rate directly proportional to the *angular size* of the prey’s image on its retina. When that neuron’s firing rate reaches a critical threshold, the dragonfly opens its legs into a capturing basket and executes the final, non‑visual “snatch”. The entire closing phase is a direct, hard‑wired reflex of blinding speed.

**Formula 343: Capture‑Basket Trigger Threshold (Angular Size)**
\[
\theta_{\text{trigger}} = \frac{S_{\text{prey}}}{d_{\text{prey}}}
\]
where \(S_{\text{prey}}\) is the wingspan of the target and \(d_{\text{prey}}\) the closing distance. At the critical angular size, the basket opens automatically.
