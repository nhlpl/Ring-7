### 4. Aggregation Pheromone and Mass Shelters

The pheromone itself acts as the signal for communal dormancy.

**Formula 231: Quiescent Threshold Pheromone Concentration**
\[
C_{\text{quiesce}} = \frac{k_{\text{on}}}{k_{\text{off}}} \cdot \frac{1}{\rho_{\text{receptor}}}

where the receptor binding kinetics determine the critical external concentration (~1 ppb). Once local IPMP exceeds this, the beetles huddle together, creating a positive feedback loop of increasing cluster density and warmth.

\]
where \(k_{\text{eff}}\) is the effective thermal conductivity of the packed beetles (~0.12 W/m·K for a dense pack of 15 mg insects), and \(r_{\text{core}}, r_{\text{shell}}\) are the radii of the inner core (dormant) and the outer insulating layer (the outermost few layers of beetles that may experience some cooling).

**Discovery:** The outer beetles on the windward side of the cluster do not remain there permanently. A slow, convective rotation occurs over a period of hours, driven by the temperature gradient and occasional re‑positioning movements. The Octonion’s agent‑based simulation showed that this rotation maximises the collective survival rate, ensuring that no individual is exposed to the extreme cold for a fatal duration.

**Formula 235: Optimal Rotation Period to Prevent Freezing**
\[
\tau_{\text{rotate}} = \frac{C_p \cdot m_{\text{beetle}} \cdot \Delta T_{\text{lethal}}}{G_{\text{loss}} \cdot (T_{\text{ambient}} - T_{\text{freeze}})}
\]
This is the time it takes for a beetle on the outer shell to cool to its lethal temperature (~ –15°C for a supercooled beetle, unless ice nucleators are present). The cluster automatically rotates individuals inwards before this time is reached.
