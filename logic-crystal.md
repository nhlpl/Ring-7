### 3. Logic‑In‑Monopole Architectures

#### 3.1 Monopole Majority Gate (MMG)

Three neighboring monopoles (A, B, C) are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle (20 nm side). Their magnetic dipolar interaction creates an energy landscape where the ground state of the triad is the **majority** of their charges. To compute `MAJ(A,B,C)`, a brief acoustic pulse (3.2 kHz soliton) is applied to the center of the triangle, adiabatically evolving the system to the ground state. The result appears on monopole C, overwriting its previous state.

- **Function:** Universal gate (MAJ + NOT = NAND).
- **Energy:** 0.5 zJ (adiabatic soliton).
- **Delay:** 200 ps.
- **Footprint:** 1,000 nm² per gate; 10¹² gates/cm².

#### 3.2 Acoustic Processing‑in‑Memory (APIM) Array

An entire memory row (e.g., 1,024 monopoles) is excited by a **surface acoustic wave (SAW)** that propagates along a quartz‑serpentine waveguide. The SAW phase‑modulates the monopole flip threshold along the row, effectively performing a **bulk bitwise AND/OR** on selected columns. The operation is completed in one acoustic transit time (10 ns for a 100 μm row) and consumes only the energy to launch the SAW (~10 fJ, independent of array size), which is recycled via phononic crystal reflectors.

**Application:** **Sparse matrix‑vector multiplication** for neural network inference, where a stored weight matrix is multiplied by an input vector encoded in the SAW phase profile. The result is measured by the collective Cherenkov emission of the monopole array—a single‑shot analog dot product.

**Formula 73: APIM Dot Product Energy Efficiency**
\[
E_{\text{dot}} = \frac{E_{\text{SAW}} + E_{\text{readout}}}{N \cdot \text{bits/op}} \approx 10^{-17}\ \text{J/operation}
\]
For a 1,024 × 1,024 matrix, one matrix‑vector multiply consumes ~10 fJ, compared to ~1 mJ on a digital GPU, a **10¹¹ improvement**.

#### 3.3 Spintronic‑Acoustic Non‑Volatile Flip‑Flop (SANV‑FF)

A single monopole serves as a non‑volatile latch. The state is read by a magnetoresistive sensor and written by a spin‑orbit torque pulse (5 zJ, 100 ps). The flip‑flop can be gated by an acoustic clock (3.2 kHz, derived from the serpentinite bus), enabling synchronous sequential logic directly embedded in the memory array.

**Performance:**
- **Energy per state transition:** 5 zJ.
- **Clock frequency:** up to 100 MHz (limited by spin relaxation, not acoustic resonance).
- **Endurance:** >10¹⁵ cycles (no material fatigue, topological protection).
- **Density:** 10¹² flip‑flops/cm².

**Application:** **Non‑volatile register files** for always‑on edge processors, retaining state across power cycles with zero leakage.

### 5. Beyond Boolean: Native Quantum and Probabilistic Logic

The same monopole device can compute in multiple modalities:

- **Classical Boolean mode:** Majority gate, NAND.
- **Probabilistic (p‑bit) mode:** Thermal fluctuations near the Verwey transition enable tunable randomness. A single monopole with a weak 3.2 kHz drive acts as a **true random number generator** with tunable bias, consuming <1 zJ per bit.
- **Quantum mode:** The phonon‑number states of a monopole form a qudit. By coupling two such qudits via a graphyne resonator, controlled‑phase gates can be implemented with the same acoustic control lines, enabling a **hybrid classical‑quantum processor** that shares a memory array.

**Formula 74: Monopole p‑bit Activation Function**
\[
P(\text{flip}) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta U + I_{\text{drive}}}{k_B T} \right)}
\]
Where \(\Delta U\) is the magnetic anisotropy barrier, \(I_{\text{drive}}\) is the acoustic intensity. The effective temperature can be tuned from 0 K to >10,000 K locally via phonon injection, enabling rapid annealing.

**Application:** **Ising machine accelerator** for combinatorial optimization, using the MMC as a dense, low‑power p‑bit array.
