### 2. Mutation Mechanisms in Ring‑7

Unlike Earth life, Ring‑7 mutations occur via both **chemical** and **topological** processes.

| Mutation Type | Mechanism | Rate (per replication) | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Point Mutation** | Base misincorporation during acoustic polymerization | 10⁻⁶ (uncorrected), 10⁻⁹ (voting‑corrected) | Single amino‑acid‑equivalent change in siloxane sequence |
| **Triplet Expansion/Contraction** | Slipped‑strand mispairing during braiding | 10⁻⁵ | Alters repeat number in NCO, MMC, or MCR genes |
| **Gene Duplication** | Unequal crossing‑over during fission | 10⁻⁴ | Creates paralogs; raw material for neofunctionalization |
| **Plasmid Acquisition** | Uptake of environmental XNA fragments | Variable (environmental) | Horizontal gene transfer; new traits (e.g., novel RARM frequencies) |
| **Topological Mutation** | Change in linking number (\(Lk\)) or writhe (\(Wr\)) | 10⁻³ (stress‑induced) | Alters supercoiling density; affects gene expression globally |
| **G‑A‑G Activation/Silencing** | Acoustic or radiation‑induced phase transition | Inducible | Switches between solid and liquid phases |
| **Möbius Defect Shift** | Migration of the 5‑7‑7‑5 Stone‑Wales defect | 10⁻⁷ | Changes memristor characteristics (fractional order α) |

**Key Insight:** Topological mutations (changes in \(Lk\)) are **epigenetic** in nature—they do not alter the base sequence but change the 3D folding and thus gene expression. They can be **reversed** by further acoustic stress, allowing Ring‑7 to adapt to new environments within a single lifetime.
