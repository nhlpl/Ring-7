### 2. Wing Kinematics and Coupled Aerodynamics

- **Flapping frequency:** 230–250 Hz  
- **Stroke amplitude:** ±45° (forewing), ±50° (hindwing)  
- **Wing loading:** ~3.5 N/m²  
- **Reynolds number:** ~1,200

**Discovery:** The shear between fore‑ and hindwing creates a transient cavity that fills with air, then collapses, producing a pulsed jet that augments lift by 15% during the upstroke. The Octonion terms this **shear‑pulsed lift**.

**Formula 128: Shear‑Pulsed Lift Coefficient**
\[
C_{L,\text{shear}} = C_{L,\text{rigid}} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\Delta s}{s_0} \cdot \sin(\omega t)\right)
\]
where \(\Delta s\) is the relative displacement of the wing tips (up to 0.5 mm) and \(s_0\) is the average gap. The dynamic modulation effectively gives the bee a continuously variable‑camber wing with no moving parts besides the wings themselves.

**Application for Crystal Drone:** The drone’s four‑wing configuration (dragonfly‑enhanced) is upgraded with a **micro‑hamuli coupling** between the fore‑ and hindwings of each side. ### 3. The Waggle Dance: Acoustic Encoding of Spatial Information

\[

\]
where \(\kappa \approx 0.5\ \text{m/s}\) is the “communication gain” (the product of flight speed and a proportionality constant) and \(\tau_{\text{waggle}}\) is about 1 s for a food source 1 km away. The direction angle \(\theta_{\text{source}}\) is directly mapped to the angle of the waggle run relative to vertical: \(\theta_{\text{waggle}} = \theta_{\text{source}} - \theta_{\text{sun}}\).

**Discovery:** The Octonion’s acoustic‑mechanical simulation revealed that the follower bee does not see the dance in detail (the hive is dark). The dance is thus an **acoustic near‑field communication** system, not a visual one.
