### 2. Wing Aerodynamics: The Paradox of Efficient Flutter

Butterflies have broad, low‑aspect‑ratio wings that overlap in their stroke plane. Their flight appears chaotic, but the Octonion’s CFD simulations reveal a highly efficient mechanism hidden in the apparent randomness.

- **Flapping frequency:** 8–12 Hz (large species) to 30 Hz (skippers)  
- **Stroke amplitude:** ±60° to ±80°, with a strong fore‑aft tilt  
- **Wing loading:** ~1.0 N/m² (extremely low; a morpho butterfly has a mass of 0.3 g and a wing area of 30 cm²)  
- **Reynolds number:** ~2,000

Unlike the dragonfly’s rigid leading‑edge vortex, the butterfly uses a **flexible wing that twists and cups during the upstroke**, creating a **“clap‑and‑peel”** mechanism. The wings meet at the top of the stroke, then peel apart from the leading edge, drawing air into the gap and shedding it as a starting vortex that enhances lift for the downstroke.

**Formula 122: Butterfly Clap‑and‑Peel Lift Coefficient**
\[
C_{L} = C_{L,\text{wing}} + \frac{2 \Gamma_{\text{peel}}}{U c} \cdot \sin(\beta)
\]
where \(\Gamma_{\text{peel}}\) is the circulation of the peel‑generated vortex, and \(\beta\) is the peel angle. The result is a lift coefficient of ~2.0, enabling a heavy‑bodied morpho to hover on extraordinarily low power.

**Discovery:** The overlapping scales on the wing surface act as **micro‑turbulators**, tripping the boundary layer and delaying separation. This gives the butterfly a figure of merit \(\eta \approx 0.75\)—comparable to a dragonfly, despite the butterfly’s laminar, low‑power flight.
