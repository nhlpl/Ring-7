### 4. Leaf‑Reinforced Thin Cocoon Engineering

Unlike the thick, double‑layered cocoons of the Polyphemus or Cecropia moths, the Luna moth caterpillar spins a remarkably thin, papery silk cocoon, often incorporating a living leaf as a structural backbone. - **Silk layer thickness:** <0.1 mm
- **Cocoon shape:** Loosely ovoid, wrapped within a brown, curled leaf.
- **Material conservation:** Uses 80% less silk protein than a comparably‑sized Saturniid cocoon.

**Discovery:** The caterpillar secretes an enzyme onto the leaf surface that accelerates its senescence, causing it to curl and turn brown before it is even incorporated into the cocoon. The leaf then serves as both a waterproof, UV‑protective shell and a perfect piece of environmental camouflage. The silk itself is merely a thin, parchment‑like inner lining that provides structural integrity.

\]
The naturally senesced leaf has a spectral reflectance that is statistically identical to the surrounding leaf litter, making the cocoon essentially invisible to ground‑foraging predators.
